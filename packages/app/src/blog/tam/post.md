# Overview

TAM is a substrate-agnostic causal model of agency. It applies to any system able to represent situations, choices, and outcomes, and offers a principled lens for defining agency and supporting experiential learning without prescribing implementation details. It can serve as a causal log of agent behavior, or as a decision-making framework whose expectations evolve through experience.

TAM is a theory of agency that defines the structural requirements for something to count as an Actor, and gives a precise meaning to what it means for that Actor to exhibit Agency.

- Actors are causal participants that interpret their own actions and the world’s responses through the port–trajectory–situation cycle.
- Agency requires being accountable to causal expectations you yourself chose. Actors exhibit agency the degree to which their choices exclude counterfactual worlds and stake predictive commitments.
